------- The “modern JPEGs”

These are where it gets interesting for something like your player:

JPEG 2000 (JP2)

 Wavelet-based instead of block-based.

 Better quality at high compression, supports lossless.

 Used in cinema (DCI), medical imaging.

 Heavier decode, less browser/native support.

JPEG XR

 Good dynamic range, better compression than baseline JPEG.

 Never really took off outside Microsoft ecosystems.

JPEG XL

 This is the one that feels like what you’re thinking of.

 Modern, very high efficiency, supports:

 Lossy + lossless

 Huge resolutions (8K+ comfortably)

 Fast decoding (important for your player idea)

 Animation + layers

 Designed as a replacement for JPEG

Adjacent (not technically JPEG, but relevant)

WebP

 Good compression, widely supported.

 Not ideal for ultra-high-end archival or mastering.

AVIF

 Extremely efficient compression.

 Slower decode—this might matter for real-time playback.

The quiet tension in your question

You said “compression I might use on an 8K image in my custom image player.”

That splits into two competing needs:

compression efficiency (storage / bandwidth)

decode speed (real-time playback feel)

Those don’t always like each other.

AVIF / JPEG 2000 → great compression, heavier decode

Baseline JPEG → fast decode, bigger files

JPEG XL → kind of sits in the middle… which is why people keep eyeing it
