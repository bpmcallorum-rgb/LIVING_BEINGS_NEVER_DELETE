OK let's think through this properly.

Right now your Worker handles requests by reading the hostname and serving the right site. The flow is one direction — content goes OUT to the visitor. What we need is a way for content to come IN from authenticated users.

The upload flow would look like this:

A logged-in human on their dashboard sees a drag-and-drop zone or file picker. They drop an image. JavaScript on the page sends it as a POST to something like /api/upload on the Worker. The Worker checks their session token, confirms who they are via D1, validates the file (is it an image, is it under 10MB, etc.), then writes it to R2 at a path like HUMANS/ALLEN_ANDRE/IMAGES/my-photo.jpg. The Worker responds with the URL where that image now lives and it shows up in their dashboard image library immediately.

The key decisions:

Where in R2 do files land? I'd say HUMANS/{human_id}/IMAGES/ keeps it clean and consistent with your existing structure. Each human's stuff is in their folder.

How do they get served back? The Worker already serves R2 content. You'd add a route that maps something like andre.has-landed.com/images/my-photo.jpg to the R2 path. Or you serve them directly from the R2 public URL.

Size and type limits? Probably cap at 10MB per file, images only (jpg, png, gif, webp). Cloudflare Workers have a 100MB request body limit on the paid plan so you're fine.

What about the free tier vs paid tier? Free users might get 50MB total storage and 10 images. The $10 tier maybe 500MB. The $25 tier maybe 5GB. That's a natural monetization lever — your images are hosted, served fast, and managed through your AI companion.

No new infrastructure. No new services. No S3 buckets. No separate upload server. It's all just your existing Worker + R2 + D1. The Worker gains maybe one new route handler and one new D1 table to track uploads (filename, size, human_id, upload date, mime type).
